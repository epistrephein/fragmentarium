The water was shut off on the first of July, from eleven until four, for reasons the cooperative's notice called network balancing.

In practice, it meant the town stopped at eleven. Women filled their basins earlier, men washed their vehicles earlier, bars kept pitchers in the fridge and served coffee with a glass of water that had become a measurable courtesy. In the afternoon, the square held only the sound of cicadas in the lime trees and the butcher's generator.

Corradi discovered that without water, people speak more quietly. He did not understand why and did not try to explain it to himself: he noted it mentally as one notes an office datum, with a certain technical satisfaction.

At quarter past four the pipes started up with a blow, a long thud that travelled from the reservoir on the hill to the taps like news. For one minute, the whole town had the same voice.
