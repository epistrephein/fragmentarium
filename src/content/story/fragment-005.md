She got off the coach with one bag, and the first thing she thought was that they had resurfaced the road: the town was quieter because the tyres no longer made the gravel sound she had called, as a child, the sound of arriving.

The funeral was at ten. There were twenty-eight people, she counted them from the third row, and of those twenty-eight she recognised nineteen. The priest read the relatives' names from a sheet of paper, in the wrong order, and no one corrected him because correcting him would have meant saying a name aloud in church.

Afterwards, in the forecourt, they held her there in the way towns do: not with questions, but with observations.

— You came back.

— For today.

— Of course, for today.

They told her about the road, the water, the committee, and each of the three things was told to her as if it were the only one. Then they told her, almost all in the same words, that nothing happened here anymore.

On the cooperative's wall, while the return coach manoeuvred, she counted twelve posters spaced so that the wall looked full, and understood that it was the one thing no one had told her.
